Nested turbulence: Tom Abel's NestedMHD on a GPU

A nest puts a finer grid inside a coarser one: here three levels, each twice as fine as the one around it, centred in a periodic box, so the central quarter of the box is computed at four times the resolution of the rest. Tom's question: does the finest level of a nest reproduce what a uniform grid at that resolution gives, for much less computing? We ran his code (NestedTurbulence, MIT) on Sherlock's H100s, which it had not run on before, fixed what stopped it in 3-D, and set a nest against a uniform grid in driven Mach-10 MHD turbulence.

The short answer so far

  • It runs, and it is cheap. The 3 × 64³ nest (finest 256³-equivalent) reached t = 1 in 225 s. Over t = 0.2–0.35 it took 26 s where the uniform 256³ grid took 68 min (158×). Constrained transport keeps div B at round-off (5e-05) on every level.
  • Large-scale statistics agree. Over the whole box the rms Mach number is 10.38 (nest) and 10.16 (uniform), and at t = 0.35 the rms field is 1.45 and 1.44. The density and speed PDFs of the central cube match.
  • Small scales do not, yet. In the central cube at the same resolution, wave numbers 32–96 hold 18 % of the density variance in the nest's finest level and 31 % in the uniform grid; for the velocity 1.2 % and 1.9 %, for the field 22 % and 25 % (figure 4).
  • The uniform grids are the slow part. The uniform 256³ run stalled at t = 0.35; the uniform 512³ run reached no output in about 2 hours. Nearly empty cells (ρ near the floor of 10⁻⁶) make the Alfvén speed, and so the time step, collapse. The 3 × 128³ nest meets them too (figure 5).

Normalized power spectra

Each field's variance per unit ln k, k E(k)/σ² (that is 4π k³P(k)/σ² for the 3-D power P(k)), so the area under a curve over ln k is 1 and runs with different total power compare by where their variance lives. Velocity, magnetic field and density, averaged over the snapshots (every 0.05). Top: the whole periodic box at the nest's root resolution. Bottom: the central cube at the finest resolution; it is not periodic, so it is multiplied by a Hann window first, which sets the lowest wave numbers (shaded). The nest 3 × 128³ is averaged to t = 0.5, the other two to the uniform run's last output; the field is still growing, so its spectra are not compared across those times.

In these runs: over the whole box, outside its finer levels the nest is a 64³ simulation: k = 8–24 holds 6.8 % of its velocity variance against 9.0 % in the uniform grid. In the central cube, at the same resolution, k = 32–96 holds 18 % of the density variance in the nest's finest level against 31 % in the uniform grid (42 % in the 3 × 128³ nest, twice as fine); the field differs less (22 % against 25 %). Gas crosses the central cube (a quarter of the box) in about 0.025 at Mach 10, probably too short for the cascade to rebuild the small scales the coarser levels never had. A wider finest level will test that.

5

Cost and time step

Left: wall-clock time on one H100. Middle: the mean time step of the finest grid between outputs. Right: the lowest density in the central cube, and over the whole uniform 256³ grid.

In these runs: to t = 0.35 the uniform 256³ grid took 133,046 steps, 7.5 times as many as the nest's finest level (17,643). Its step fell to 3e-07 while the 3 × 64³ nest's never went below 6e-06. The cause is nearly empty cells: where ρ approaches the floor (10⁻⁶) the Alfvén speed B/√ρ becomes huge. The 3 × 128³ nest, with a finer finest level, meets them as well (its step fell to 8e-08 near t = 0.29), so they come with resolution, not with the uniform grid itself.

Set-up

Box of side 1, mean density 1, sound speed 1, isothermal. A uniform field along x with plasma β = 200 (B₀ = 0.1). A solenoidal random acceleration on 1 ≤ |k| ≤ 3, scaled continuously to hold the rms Mach number at 10, random seed 1 in every run. Tom's MUSCL-Hancock scheme with HLL fluxes and constrained transport, Courant 0.25, density floor 10⁻⁶. The nests: each level has as many cells as the root (64³ or 128³) and covers the central half of the level around it; each level takes its own time step (Berger–Oliger), with refluxing and an EMF correction at the level boundaries, so mass and div B = 0 are kept.

What it took to run in 3-D on a GPU

  • The GPU agrees with the CPU to 3e-08 in double precision and 3e-06 in single, with the same number of steps on every level (a nested Orszag–Tang, CT and GLM). Speed: a uniform grid 1.6–2.1 × 10⁹ cell updates per second in single precision; the 3 × 128³ nest 0.37 × 10⁹ with CT and 1.09 × 10⁹ with GLM.
  • Courant 0.4 is unstable in 3-D with these kernels, even on a uniform grid (they add the three directions' signal speeds); 0.25 is used. Tom's own runs were 2-D.
  • Ghost cells could go negative. Filling a fine level's ghost cells from its parent added three independently limited slopes, so next to a nearly empty cell a corner ghost could reach −½ of its parent's density; every 3-D nest failed at t ≈ 0.034. Fixed by one factor that scales all the slopes of a cell so the fine density stays above the lowest of its six coarse neighbours.
  • Faster driving. The driving's totals are now summed on the GPU instead of copying the root level to the CPU every step.

The fixes are on our branch gpu-3d-fixes of his code; they are not in Tom's repository yet.

Open

  • A fair uniform reference at 256³ and 512³: either a longer run, or the same safeguard for nearly empty cells in both (a higher floor, or a cap on the Alfvén speed).
  • Why the finest level is short of small-scale power: a larger finest level or more levels would tell.
  • Longer runs of the nests, for time-averaged statistics once the field has saturated.
