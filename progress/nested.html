Nested turbulence: Tom Abel's NestedMHD on a GPU

A nest puts a finer grid inside a coarser one: here three or four levels, each twice as fine as the one around it, centred in a periodic box, so the centre of the box is computed at four or eight times the resolution of the rest. Tom's question: does the finest level of a nest reproduce what a uniform grid at that resolution gives, for much less computing? We ran his code (NestedTurbulence, MIT) on Sherlock's H100s, which it had not run on before, fixed what stopped it in 3-D, and set nests against uniform grids in driven Mach-10 MHD turbulence.

The short answer

  • The nest is 13–17 times faster to the same time. With the same cap on the Alfvén speed in both (30 cs; it adds at most 2e-04 of the mass), every run now reaches its end: the 3 × 64³ nest t = 1 in 214 s against 61 min for the uniform 256³ grid, the 3 × 128³ nest t = 0.5 in 21 min against 4.3 h for the uniform 512³ grid, with 50 and 49 times fewer cell updates (figure 2).
  • Density agrees. Over the central cube from t = 0.3 to 1, ln ρ has mean -1.24 and spread 1.63 in the nest and -1.19 and 1.62 in the uniform grid, and k = 32–96 holds 33 % and 35 % of the density variance. The gap we reported first (18 % against 31 %) came from the early outputs alone: before t ≈ 0.35 the finest level has not yet built its small scales.
  • Velocity may be short at small scales; it takes a window of time to tell. Each snapshot's spectrum divided by its variance in the central cube, then averaged over every snapshot from t = 0.3 on (one per crossing time): over k = 8–96 the nest's velocity is 0.72 of the uniform grid's at 256³ (95 %: 0.52–0.97, 15 snapshots) and 0.75 at 512³ (0.46–1.28, 5 snapshots); field and density are within their intervals of 1. A single pair of snapshots at one time gives anything from 0.1 to 2.4 for the velocity: compared at one instant the two grids can look equal or very different (figure 3). Longer windows are set up (to t = 3 at 256³, 1.5 at 512³).
  • One point, over time, samples the volume (ergodicity). In 8 runs with different driving seeds, the time mean of ln ρ at one point is -1.30 and the volume mean -1.25 (0.8 standard errors apart); the mass-weighted mean, +1.05, is far off. The correlation time at the point is 0.009, about a tenth of a crossing time (figure 4).
  • A big nest runs too. Root 128³ plus three levels of 192³ (1024³-equivalent in the central 3/16 of the box) reached t = 0.5 in 2.2 h on one H100 (figure 5).

2

Nest against uniform grid, both capped

The comparison again, now with the same safeguard in both: where the Alfvén speed B/√ρ would exceed 30 cs, the density is raised to bring it down, at the same velocity. Dashed: finest resolution 256³ (3 × 64³ nest and uniform 256³, to t = 1). Solid: finest 512³ (3 × 128³ nest and uniform 512³, to t = 0.5). Top: each field's variance per unit ln k in the central cube (Hann-windowed, the shaded wave numbers are set by the window), averaged over the snapshots from t = 0.3 on. Bottom: the density PDF of the central cube over the same outputs, the central cube's rms field, and the wall-clock time.

In these runs: the density PDFs lie on top of each other at 256³; at 512³ the nest's is a little narrower (1.48 against 1.61) over only 5 snapshots. The velocity spectra of the nests fall below the uniform grids' from k ≈ 15 on, by about 25 % at 256³ and 17 % at 512³: the finest level is fed through its boundary by coarser levels that do not carry those scales. The central cube's rms field swings by a factor of 3–4 in time in every run, so its spectra need longer runs than these to compare. The cap adds 2e-04 of the mass in the uniform 512³ grid and 1e-05 or less in the others.

3

Spectra over a window of time, with their errors

The capped runs again, every snapshot from t = 0.3 on (one per crossing time, t = L/(2·10 cs) = 0.05). Each snapshot's spectrum in the central cube is divided by its own variance there (the cube's total variance swings with the large eddies passing through it), then the snapshots are averaged. Intervals: bootstrap over the snapshots of each run (neighbouring snapshots are uncorrelated: lag-1 correlation -0.28 to +0.12). Top: the windowed spectra, 95 % intervals. Middle and bottom: the nest's over the uniform grid's for each pair; dark, the windowed ratio with its 95 % interval; pale, the 10–90 % range of the ratio of one snapshot pair taken at the same time.

In these runs: averaged over the window, the nests' velocity over k = 8–96 is 0.72 of the uniform grid's at 256³ (95 %: 0.52–0.97) and 0.75 at 512³ (0.46–1.28): short at 256³, just outside the interval, and not measurable at 512³ with 5 snapshots. The field (0.78 and 1.01) and density (0.80 and 0.98) are within their intervals of 1. One pair of snapshots at one time puts the velocity ratio anywhere from 0.11 to 2.45, and the raw power (not divided by the variance) from 0.08 to 4.1: the comparison only means something over a window. To shrink the interval by two needs four times the snapshots: the long runs (55 snapshots at 256³, 25 at 512³) are ready to submit.

4

Ergodicity: one point over time against the volume

Tom's test (his report, chapter 7) in 3-D: 8 runs of the 3 × 64³ nest to t = 1, identical but for the random seed of the driving, each with a probe in the finest-level cell at the box centre, read every second step of that level. Left: the PDF of s = ln ρ at the probe over time (all seeds pooled) and over the finest level's volume. Middle: per seed, the probe's time mean of s with its error σ/√Neff, where Neff = T/2τint (about 41 independent samples per run), against the volume mean of the finest level and the mass-weighted mean. Right: the autocorrelation of s at the probe.

In these runs: the time mean matches the volume mean within its error in 8 of 8 runs, and the 8 time means scatter by 0.19, no more than the errors predict (0.26). So a point in the turbulence samples the volume-weighted distribution, not the mass-weighted one (mean +1.05). The correlation time, 0.009, is about a tenth of the crossing time L / (10 cs) = 0.1. Each run took 3.5 min.

Normalized power spectra

Each field's variance per unit ln k, k E(k)/σ² (that is 4π k³P(k)/σ² for the 3-D power P(k)), so the area under a curve over ln k is 1 and runs with different total power compare by where their variance lives. Velocity, magnetic field and density, averaged over the snapshots (every 0.05). Top: the whole periodic box at the nest's root resolution. Bottom: the central cube at the finest resolution; it is not periodic, so it is multiplied by a Hann window first, which sets the lowest wave numbers (shaded). The nest 3 × 128³ is averaged to t = 0.5, the other two to the uniform run's last output; the field is still growing, so its spectra are not compared across those times.

In these runs: over the whole box, outside its finer levels the nest is a 64³ simulation: k = 8–24 holds 6.8 % of its velocity variance against 9.0 % in the uniform grid. In the central cube, at the same resolution, k = 32–96 holds 18 % of the density variance in the nest's finest level against 31 % in the uniform grid (42 % in the 3 × 128³ nest, twice as fine); the field differs less (22 % against 25 %). Gas crosses the central cube (a quarter of the box) in about 0.025 at Mach 10, probably too short for the cascade to rebuild the small scales the coarser levels never had. The capped runs to t = 1 (figure 2) show the density gap closes after t ≈ 0.35; the velocity stays somewhat short.

9

Cost and time step

Left: wall-clock time on one H100. Middle: the mean time step of the finest grid between outputs. Right: the lowest density in the central cube, and over the whole uniform 256³ grid.

In these runs: to t = 0.35 the uniform 256³ grid took 133,046 steps, 7.5 times as many as the nest's finest level (17,643). Its step fell to 3e-07 while the 3 × 64³ nest's never went below 6e-06. The cause is nearly empty cells: where ρ approaches the floor (10⁻⁶) the Alfvén speed B/√ρ becomes huge. The 3 × 128³ nest, with a finer finest level, meets them as well (its step fell to 8e-08 near t = 0.29), so they come with resolution, not with the uniform grid itself.

Set-up

Box of side 1, mean density 1, sound speed 1, isothermal. A uniform field along x with plasma β = 200 (B₀ = 0.1). A solenoidal random acceleration on 1 ≤ |k| ≤ 3, scaled continuously to hold the rms Mach number at 10, random seed 1 in every run. Tom's MUSCL-Hancock scheme with HLL fluxes and constrained transport, Courant 0.25, density floor 10⁻⁶. The nests: each level has as many cells as the root (64³ or 128³) and covers the central half of the level around it; each level takes its own time step (Berger–Oliger), with refluxing and an EMF correction at the level boundaries, so mass and div B = 0 are kept.

What it took to run in 3-D on a GPU

  • The GPU agrees with the CPU to 3e-08 in double precision and 3e-06 in single, with the same number of steps on every level (a nested Orszag–Tang, CT and GLM). Speed: a uniform grid 1.6–2.1 × 10⁹ cell updates per second in single precision; the 3 × 128³ nest 0.37 × 10⁹ with CT and 1.09 × 10⁹ with GLM.
  • Courant 0.4 is unstable in 3-D with these kernels, even on a uniform grid (they add the three directions' signal speeds); 0.25 is used. Tom's own runs were 2-D.
  • Ghost cells could go negative. Filling a fine level's ghost cells from its parent added three independently limited slopes, so next to a nearly empty cell a corner ghost could reach −½ of its parent's density; every 3-D nest failed at t ≈ 0.034. Fixed by one factor that scales all the slopes of a cell so the fine density stays above the lowest of its six coarse neighbours.
  • Faster driving. The driving's totals are now summed on the GPU instead of copying the root level to the CPU every step.

The fixes are on our branch gpu-3d-fixes of his code; they are not in Tom's repository yet.

Open

  • Why the finest level is short of small-scale velocity: a run whose finest level is wider than a quarter of the box, against a uniform grid at its resolution, would tell whether it is the time gas spends inside the level.
  • Longer windows for the time-averaged spectra: capped runs to t = 3 at 256³ (55 snapshots) and t = 1.5 at 512³ (25), set up in compare.jl (cases *_long), not yet submitted. With 15 and 5 snapshots the 95 % intervals of the spectrum ratios are ±25–50 %.
