What each cell stores

Read the nine slots
Matching equations

Bold v and B each stand for three components, giving nine slots in each tuple.

LaTeX source
\[ \mathbf{U}=(\rho,\rho\mathbf{v},E,\mathbf{B},\psi) \]
\[ \mathbf{q}=(\rho,\mathbf{v},p,\mathbf{B},\psi) \]

Slots 2–4 change from momentum density ρv to velocity v; slot 5 changes from total energy density E to gas pressure p. B and ψ keep their slots. Magnetic units absorb μ₀, giving |B|²/2 for magnetic energy density.

Recover pressure from energy
Matching equations

m = (c[2], c[3], c[4]); smallr = ρmin; pfl = pmin. K and M correspond to ekin and emag.

LaTeX source
\[ \rho=\max(U_1,\rho_{\min}),\quad\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{m}/\rho \]
\[ K=\frac{|\mathbf{m}|^2}{2\rho},\quad M=\frac{|\mathbf{B}|^2}{2} \]
\[ p=\max\!\left[(\gamma-1)(E-K-M),p_{\min}\right] \]

Subtract kinetic and magnetic energy from E to recover internal energy, then multiply by γ−1. max(..., pfl) imposes a pressure floor on this primitive state. It does not by itself rewrite the stored conserved energy.

Convert back
Matching equations

Magnetic units absorb μ₀. This definition of E has no additional ψ-energy term.

LaTeX source
\[ E=\frac{p}{\gamma-1}+\frac{\rho|\mathbf{v}|^2}{2}+\frac{|\mathbf{B}|^2}{2} \]
\[ \mathbf{m}=\rho\mathbf{v} \]

Build total energy from gas pressure, kinetic energy, and magnetic energy. Multiply velocities by density to obtain momentum densities. All these quantities are per volume, rather than totals for the whole cell.

02

The equations become a flux

Source ↗

This is the ideal-MHD flux through an x face for one state. Later, the Riemann solver combines the left and right states into one numerical face flux.

98@inline function phys_flux_x(q::NTuple{9}, gamma::Float32)99    rho = q[1]; vx = q[2]; vy = q[3]; vz = q[4]; p = q[5]100    bx = q[6]; by = q[7]; bz = q[8]101    b2 = bx*bx + by*by + bz*bz102    ptot = p + 0.5f0*b2103    ekin = 0.5f0*rho*(vx*vx + vy*vy + vz*vz)104    E = p/(gamma - 1.0f0) + ekin + 0.5f0*b2105    vdotb = vx*bx + vy*by + vz*bz106    return (rho*vx,107            rho*vx*vx + ptot - bx*bx,108            rho*vx*vy - bx*by,109            rho*vx*vz - bx*bz,110            (E + ptot)*vx - bx*vdotb,111            zero(rho),              # F[Bx] -> set by glm_pair112            vx*by - vy*bx,113            vx*bz - vz*bx,114            zero(rho))              # F[psi] -> set by glm_pair115end
Pressure includes the magnetic field
Matching equations

ptot = total pressure; vdotb = v·B. The negative magnetic products are the tension terms.

LaTeX source
\[ p_{\rm tot}=p+\frac{|\mathbf{B}|^2}{2} \]
\[ F_{m_x}=\rho v_x^2+p_{\rm tot}-B_x^2 \]
\[ F_{m_y}=\rho v_xv_y-B_xB_y \]
\[ F_E=(E+p_{\rm tot})v_x-B_x(\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{B}) \]

ptot is p + |B|²/2. The −Bx² and −BxBy terms in the momentum flux encode directional magnetic stresses. The energy flux also includes magnetic work through −Bx(v·B).

Nine flux components
Matching equations

⊥ means the y and z components at an x face. The last two fluxes are supplied by glm_pair, replacing the placeholders.

LaTeX source
\[ F_\rho=\rho v_x \]
\[ \mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{B}_\perp}=v_x\mathbf{B}_\perp-B_x\mathbf{v}_\perp \]
\[ F_{B_x}=\psi^*,\quad F_\psi=c_h^2 B_x^* \]

The tuple follows the conserved-state order. Its first entry is mass flux ρvx. The Bx and ψ entries are placeholders: the GLM pair fills them separately, while the transverse magnetic fluxes come from the induction equation.

Reconstruct the cell edges

Source ↗

PLM estimates a line inside each cell. MonCen limits that line so sharp jumps do not produce large new overshoots.

Compare the two neighboring differences
Matching equations

dl, dr, dc map to dL, dR, dC. The zero-slope rule overrides the preceding expression at an extremum.

LaTeX source
\[ d_L=q_i-q_{i-1},\quad d_R=q_{i+1}-q_i \]
\[ d_C=\frac{d_L+d_R}{2} \]
\[ \sigma={\rm sgn}(d_C)\min(2|d_L|,2|d_R|,|d_C|) \]
\[ \sigma=0\quad\mathrm{if}\quad d_Ld_R\leq0 \]

dl = M−L and dr = R−M. If their signs disagree, the cell is at an extremum and the limiter returns zero. Otherwise it caps the centered slope using twice the smaller one-sided difference.

These are half-slopes
Matching equations

prim_slope returns s, the half-slope. padd(q, s, ±1) builds the two spatial edges, component by component.

LaTeX source
\[ s_i=\frac{1}{2}\sigma_i \]
\[ q_{i,L}=q_i-s_i,\quad q_{i,R}=q_i+s_i \]

prim_slope applies MonCen to all nine primitive quantities, then multiplies by 1/2. That is why padd(q, s, +1) reaches one face and padd(q, s, −1) reaches the other; s already includes the half-cell distance.

04

Predict half a timestep

Source ↗

The Hancock predictor advances the reconstructed cell state to the middle of the timestep, before neighboring states meet at a face.

165@fastmath @inline function hancock(m0::NTuple{9}, sx, sy, sz, dtdx::Float32, gamma::Float32)166    u0 = prim2cons(m0, gamma)167    h = 0.5f0*dtdx168    fxp = dir_flux(padd(m0, sx, 1.0f0), 1, gamma); fxm = dir_flux(padd(m0, sx, -1.0f0), 1, gamma)169    fyp = dir_flux(padd(m0, sy, 1.0f0), 2, gamma); fym = dir_flux(padd(m0, sy, -1.0f0), 2, gamma)170    fzp = dir_flux(padd(m0, sz, 1.0f0), 3, gamma); fzm = dir_flux(padd(m0, sz, -1.0f0), 3, gamma)171    return ntuple(i -> u0[i] - h*((fxp[i]-fxm[i]) + (fyp[i]-fym[i]) + (fzp[i]-fzm[i])), 9)172end
The half-step
Matching equations

h is the code variable h. fxp−fxm, fyp−fym, and fzp−fzm are the three directional differences.

LaTeX source
\[ h=\frac{\Delta t}{2\Delta x} \]
\[ \Delta\mathbf{F}_d=\mathbf{F}_d(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{s}_d)-\mathbf{F}_d(\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{s}_d) \]
\[ \mathbf{U}^{n+1/2}=\mathbf{U}^n-h\sum_{d=x,y,z}\Delta\mathbf{F}_d \]

dtdx means Δt/Δx. Multiplying by 0.5 gives the half-step factor. Each direction contributes a difference of physical fluxes evaluated at that cell’s reconstructed edges.

All directions contribute together
Matching equations

The predictor sums all directions at once. The second line shows the later face construction; floors or fallback may modify those states.

LaTeX source
\[ \mathbf{U}^{n+1/2}=\mathbf{U}^n-\frac{\Delta t}{2\Delta x}\sum_d\Delta\mathbf{F}_d \]
\[ \mathbf{q}_{d,\pm}^{n+1/2}=\mathbf{q}^{n+1/2}\pm\mathbf{s}_d \]

The x, y, and z differences enter a single predicted conserved state. This is the unsplit predictor used here. These are local physical fluxes for prediction; the later corrector uses numerical Riemann fluxes shared by cells.

05

Solve the GLM cleaning pair

Source ↗

Before the physical MHD solve, the code reconciles the left and right normal magnetic fields using the auxiliary cleaning field ψ.

175@inline function glm_pair(bnL, bnR, psiL, psiR, ch::Float32)176    bns  = 0.5f0*(bnL + bnR) - 0.5f0*(psiR - psiL)/ch177    psis = 0.5f0*(psiL + psiR) - 0.5f0*ch*(bnR - bnL)178    return bns, psis179end
One shared normal field
Matching equations

bns = Bn*; psis = ψ*. A star marks an interface solution, not a time derivative.

LaTeX source
\[ B_n^*=\frac{B_{n,L}+B_{n,R}}{2}-\frac{\psi_R-\psi_L}{2c_h} \]
\[ \psi^*=\frac{\psi_L+\psi_R}{2}-\frac{c_h}{2}(B_{n,R}-B_{n,L}) \]

bns is Bn*, the interface magnetic field after solving the two-wave linear cleaning subsystem. A jump in ψ changes this field; a jump in Bn changes ψ*. Both sides of the following MHD solve receive the same Bn*.

What cleaning transports
Matching equations

These are the linear cleaning equations before damping, with ch held constant during the face solve.

LaTeX source
\[ \partial_t B_n+\partial_n\psi=0 \]
\[ \partial_t\psi+c_h^2\partial_n B_n=0 \]
\[ \lambda_{\rm GLM}=\pm c_h \]

The pair corresponds to ∂t Bn + ∂n ψ = 0 and ∂t ψ + ch² ∂n Bn = 0 before damping, with wave speeds ±ch. It controls numerical divergence errors; it does not impose exact discrete ∇·B = 0.

06

Estimate how fast information travels

Source ↗

The fast magnetosonic speed is relative to the gas. The Riemann solver adds the fluid velocity to estimate speeds relative to the fixed grid.

89@fastmath @inline function fast_speed(q::NTuple{9}, gamma::Float32, bn)90    rho = q[1]91    c2 = gamma * q[5] / rho92    b2 = (q[6]*q[6] + q[7]*q[7] + q[8]*q[8]) / rho93    d2 = 0.5f0*(b2 + c2)94    return sqrt(d2 + sqrt(max(d2*d2 - c2*bn*bn/rho, 0.0f0)))95end
Sound and Alfvén contributions
Matching equations

c2 = cs², b2 = vA², and d2 = D. bn is the field component normal to this face.

LaTeX source
\[ c_s^2=\frac{\gamma p}{\rho},\quad v_A^2=\frac{|\mathbf{B}|^2}{\rho} \]
\[ v_{A,n}^2=\frac{B_n^2}{\rho},\quad D=\frac{c_s^2+v_A^2}{2} \]

c2 is cs² = γp/ρ; b2 is vA² = |B|²/ρ in these units. bn is the magnetic component normal to this face, so the result depends on the propagation direction.

Choose the fast root
Matching equations

D is d2 in the source. cf is measured relative to the gas; λf,± are physical fast-wave speeds in the fixed-grid frame.

LaTeX source
\[ D=\frac{c_s^2+v_A^2}{2} \]
\[ c_f=\sqrt{D+\sqrt{\max(D^2-c_s^2v_{A,n}^2,0)}} \]
\[ \lambda_{f,\pm}=v_n\pm c_f \]

The nested square roots give the fast branch of the magnetosonic relation. The max prevents a small negative discriminant from roundoff. Parallel to B, cf = max(cs, vA); perpendicular to B, cf = √(cs²+vA²).

07

LLF: average flux plus diffusion

Source ↗

This is the Rusanov / local Lax–Friedrichs flux used by the original benchmark path. L and R are reconstructed, predicted primitive states with a shared cleaned normal field.

182@fastmath @inline function llf_x(L::NTuple{9}, R::NTuple{9}, gamma::Float32, ch::Float32,183                       fbn, fpsi)184    fL = phys_flux_x(L, gamma); fR = phys_flux_x(R, gamma)185    uL = prim2cons(L, gamma);   uR = prim2cons(R, gamma)186    smax = max(abs(L[2]) + fast_speed(L, gamma, L[6]),187               abs(R[2]) + fast_speed(R, gamma, R[6]), ch)188    f = ntuple(i -> 0.5f0*(fL[i] + fR[i]) - 0.5f0*smax*(uR[i] - uL[i]), 9)189    return (f[1], f[2], f[3], f[4], f[5], fbn, f[7], f[8], fpsi)190end
Choose one speed bound
Matching equations

a is smax. Both physical fast signals and the cleaning speed enter this implementation's LLF bound.

LaTeX source
\[ a=\max(|v_{n,L}|+c_{f,L},\ |v_{n,R}|+c_{f,R},\ c_h) \]

smax bounds the absolute fluid-frame-shifted fast signals on both sides and also includes ch. A larger bound increases the coefficient of numerical diffusion in this LLF implementation.

The whole LLF formula
Matching equations

fL/fR are physical fluxes; uL/uR are conserved states; a = smax. A hat denotes a numerical face flux. GLM entries are overwritten next.

LaTeX source
\[ \widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{\rm LLF}=\frac{\mathbf{F}_L+\mathbf{F}_R}{2}-\frac{a}{2}(\mathbf{U}_R-\mathbf{U}_L) \]

The first term averages the two physical fluxes. The second subtracts half the signal bound times the conserved-state jump. It damps unresolved jumps; uR−uL is a difference of conserved states, not primitive states.

Restore the cleaning fluxes
Matching equations

fbn = ψ* and fpsi = ch²Bn*. These replace entries 6 and 9 in the x-normal tuple.

LaTeX source
\[ \widehat{F}_{B_n}=\psi^*,\quad\widehat{F}_\psi=c_h^2 B_n^* \]

The Bn and ψ flux entries are replaced with fbn = ψ* and fpsi = ch²Bn*. The physical MHD calculation and the GLM pair together supply all nine entries.

08

HLL: keep two outer wave speeds

Source ↗

HLL approximates the physical fan with two outer waves and one state between them. This source computes the two bounds directly from the left and right fast speeds.

335@fastmath @inline function hll_x(L::NTuple{9}, R::NTuple{9}, gamma::Float32, bn::Float32, fbn, fpsi)336    cfL = fast_speed(L, gamma, bn); cfR = fast_speed(R, gamma, bn)337    SL = min(min(L[2]-cfL, R[2]-cfR), 0.0f0); SR = max(max(L[2]+cfL, R[2]+cfR), 0.0f0)338    fL = phys_flux_x(L, gamma); fR = phys_flux_x(R, gamma)339    uL = prim2cons(L, gamma);   uR = prim2cons(R, gamma)340    ihd = 1.0f0/(SR - SL)341    f = ntuple(i -> (SR*fL[i] - SL*fR[i] + SL*SR*(uR[i]-uL[i]))*ihd, 9)342    return (f[1],f[2],f[3],f[4],f[5], fbn, f[7], f[8], fpsi)343end
Left and right bounds
Matching equations

SL and SR bound the physical MHD fan after GLM has supplied a shared Bn*. These are this function's actual speed estimates.

LaTeX source
\[ S_L=\min(v_{n,L}-c_{f,L},v_{n,R}-c_{f,R},0) \]
\[ S_R=\max(v_{n,L}+c_{f,L},v_{n,R}+c_{f,R},0) \]

SL takes the smallest left-going estimate; SR takes the largest right-going estimate. Clamping SL ≤ 0 and SR ≥ 0 lets one expression also give the upwind physical flux when the physical waves all travel in the same direction.

The HLL numerical flux
Matching equations

ihd = 1/(SR−SL). The LLF identity assumes the same a and the same subsequent GLM flux replacement.

LaTeX source
\[ \widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{\rm HLL}=\frac{S_R\mathbf{F}_L-S_L\mathbf{F}_R+S_LS_R(\mathbf{U}_R-\mathbf{U}_L)}{S_R-S_L} \]
\[ S_L=-a,\ S_R=a\ \Longrightarrow\ \widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{\rm HLL}=\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{\rm LLF} \]

The expression weights the left and right physical fluxes by the two wave bounds and adds a conserved-jump term. LLF is recovered for symmetric bounds SL = −a and SR = +a, with the same a and cleaning treatment.

The outer fast waves
Matching equations

vL and vR denote normal velocities (uL and uR inside hlld_x), rather than the full vectors.

LaTeX source
\[ S_L=\min[\min(v_L,v_R)-\max(c_{f,L},c_{f,R}),0] \]

The contact wave
Matching equations

den = AR−AL; SM = contact speed. The source additionally floors each star density at 10⁻¹².

LaTeX source
\[ A_L=\rho_L(S_L-v_L),\quad A_R=\rho_R(S_R-v_R) \]
\[ S_M=\frac{A_Rv_R-A_Lv_L-p_{{\rm tot},R}+p_{{\rm tot},L}}{A_R-A_L} \]

SM estimates the contact speed and pts the shared star-region total pressure. dLs and dRs are the densities behind the outer fast waves, obtained from the jump conditions.

Two internal Alfvén waves
Matching equations

SLs and SRs are the rotational Alfvén speeds. The last line is the intended five-wave ordering for physical intermediate states.

LaTeX source
\[ S_L^*=S_M-\frac{|B_n|}{\sqrt{\rho_L^*}} \]
\[ S_R^*=S_M+\frac{|B_n|}{\sqrt{\rho_R^*}} \]
\[ S_L\leq S_L^*\leq S_M\leq S_R^*\leq S_R \]

SLs and SRs add the rotational waves on either side of the contact. HLLD retains five waves in the physical MHD fan; it does not explicitly restore both slow magnetosonic waves.

Assemble one face solve

Source ↗

The original dispatcher rotates any face into an x-normal frame, applies floors and GLM cleaning, selects LLF or HLLD, then rotates the flux back. The production HLL path has its own dispatcher with the same cleaning pattern.

Rotate components, not the mesh
Matching equations

Pn is the component permutation implemented by rot_to. rot_flux_from undoes it; the grid itself does not rotate.

LaTeX source
\[ \mathbf{q}'=P_n\mathbf{q} \]
\[ \widehat{\mathbf{F}}_n=P_n^{-1}\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_x(\mathbf{q}'_L,\mathbf{q}'_R) \]

rot_to permutes velocity and magnetic components so the same x-flux routine can serve y and z faces. These are coordinate permutations; the Eulerian grid stays fixed.

Make the two interface states physical
Matching equations

L and R receive floors; Lc and Rc then receive the common normal magnetic field returned by glm_pair.

LaTeX source
\[ \rho_{L,R}\leftarrow\max(\rho_{L,R},\rho_{\min}) \]
\[ p_{L,R}\leftarrow\max(p_{L,R},p_{\min}) \]
\[ B_{n,L}^{\rm clean}=B_{n,R}^{\rm clean}=B_n^* \]

The reconstructed density and pressure are floored before solving. Cleaning supplies Bn*, ψ*, and their two fluxes. Lc and Rc then share the same normal magnetic field.

Select the physical solver
Matching equations

Each threshold rule is enabled only if its configured threshold is positive. use_hlld=false also forces LLF; otherwise the dispatcher selects HLLD.

LaTeX source
\[ \min(\rho_L,\rho_R)<\rho_{\rm switch}\ \Longrightarrow\ {\rm LLF} \]
\[ \min(p_L,p_R)<p_{\rm switch}\ \Longrightarrow\ {\rm LLF} \]

use_hlld=false forces LLF; otherwise positive density/pressure thresholds can request LLF in configured low-state regions. This is a preselected threshold fallback, not a general retry after every invalid HLLD result.

11

Update the cell by flux balance

Conservation in one line
Matching equations

u0 = old cell state; r1 = flux-updated state. The minus sign here equals the code's plus sign multiplying incoming minus outgoing flux.

LaTeX source
\[ \mathbf{U}_i^{n+1}=\mathbf{U}_i^n-\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}\sum_d\Delta\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{i,d} \]
\[ \Delta\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{i,d}=\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{i+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{e}_d}-\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{i-\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{e}_d} \]

Fxl−Fxh is the net incoming x flux, and likewise for y and z. Multiplying by Δt/Δx gives the cell-average change. Neighboring cells use the shared face flux with opposite signs, so internal contributions cancel in a domain sum up to numerical rounding.

A floor can change conserved totals
Matching equations

Applied only when the flux-updated density is below smallr. B and ψ are retained during this cell reset.

Where the timestep comes from

The benchmark driver takes the maximum over cells of Σd=x,y,z(|vd| + cf,d), then uses Δt = C Δx / that maximum. The main sweep used C = 0.25 and a unit box. Its constant-ch variant holds the initial cleaning speed fixed and adds a cleaning timestep cap.

This is driver behavior in sherlock_sweep/src/run_suite.jl (signals scan and timestep), separate from the unmodified solver excerpts above.

The rate a conserved quantity crosses a unit area in a given direction, for a single state. A Riemann flux combines the two reconstructed states at a face.

Magnetic pressure / tension

The flux uses total pressure p + |B|²/2 and directional magnetic terms such as −Bx² and −BxBy. Magnetic pressure resists compression; tension couples the direction of the field to forces and transport.

PLM reconstruction

Piecewise linear method: estimate a limited slope in each cell, then extrapolate to its two faces. In this code prim_slope returns half the cell-to-cell slope, so q ± s are the edge states.

Slope limiter / MonCen

Monotonized-central limiting keeps the centered slope when it is safe, reduces steep slopes, and sets the slope to zero when the two one-sided differences change sign. It helps prevent spurious oscillations.

Returns zero for opposite-sign differences; otherwise keeps the smaller magnitude with their common sign. The optional lean LLF path uses minmod. The original non-lean and production PLM paths shown here use MonCen.

In the setting where a method is TVD, the sum of absolute jumps between neighboring values does not grow. A familiar scalar TVD limiter does not by itself prove that the full multidimensional MHD algorithm is TVD.

Reconstruct spatial edges and evolve the cell state by half a timestep. The predicted state is used to build the left and right face states for the Riemann solve.

Contributions from x, y, and z enter one predictor and one final update. Dimensional splitting would advance the solution in successive directional substeps.

Generalized Lagrange Multiplier cleaning adds ψ. Coupling ψ to the normal magnetic field transports divergence errors; damping reduces ψ. This controls ∇·B errors rather than keeping a discrete divergence exactly zero.

ψ (psi)

The auxiliary GLM cleaning field: component 9 in both state tuples. At a face, F[Bn] = ψ* and F[ψ] = ch² Bn*. The cell update then damps ψ.

The component perpendicular to the face: Bx at an x face, By at a y face, Bz at a z face. The GLM solve supplies a shared interface value Bn* to both sides of the MHD solve.

Fast magnetosonic speed cf

The speed of the fastest physical ideal-MHD wave family relative to the gas in a chosen direction. It combines gas and magnetic restoring forces and depends on the angle to B. Add the normal fluid velocity to obtain grid-frame signal speeds.

Estimated speeds of the edges or internal waves of the Riemann fan, measured in the grid frame. HLL uses two outer estimates; LLF uses a single maximum absolute bound.

See it in the code ↑
Riemann problem / solver

Two constant states meet at a face. Their evolution forms a wave pattern; an approximate solver estimates the flux through that face from the two states.

See it in the code ↑
Wave fan

The wave pattern emerging from an initial jump. Ideal MHD has seven physical characteristic families: two fast, two Alfvén, two slow, and the contact. GLM adds its cleaning pair. Approximate solvers retain only part of this structure.

LLF / Rusanov

Local Lax–Friedrichs flux: average the physical fluxes and subtract a term proportional to the state jump and a local maximum signal speed. That second term supplies numerical diffusion.

Harten–Lax–van Leer flux: approximate the fan with two outer waves and a single intermediate state. The HLL function here estimates those speeds from the left and right fast magnetosonic speeds.

HLLE

The Einfeldt variant of HLL uses wave-speed estimates designed for robust, positivity-preserving behavior under appropriate conditions. The function on this page is named HLL; it should not be relabeled HLLE without checking its estimates and guarantees.

Contact discontinuity / HLLC

A contact travels with the normal fluid velocity and can carry a density jump. HLLC restores a contact wave in hydrodynamics. These benchmark GLM configurations use LLF, HLL, or HLLD, rather than HLLC.

See it in the code ↑
HLLD

An MHD approximate solver retaining five waves: two outer fast waves, two rotational Alfvén waves, and a contact, with four intermediate states. It does not resolve both slow-wave families separately.

See it in the code ↑
Alfvén / rotational waves

Waves that couple transverse velocity and magnetic-field changes through magnetic tension. In the HLLD intermediate states, their speeds are SM ± |Bn|/√ρ* in the code's magnetic units.

See it in the code ↑
Conservation form

Update a cell with incoming flux minus outgoing flux. A shared face flux leaves one cell and enters the next, so internal faces cancel in a global sum, up to rounding. Boundary fluxes, forcing, and numerical resets must also be counted.

Courant number / CFL timestep

Controls how far information may travel during one step relative to the cell width. The benchmark driver sums |v| + cf over all three directions before choosing Δt, so its numerical Courant value is not directly equivalent to every other code's convention.

A block of threads loads nearby cells and reuses them from fast on-chip memory. Neighboring halo cells supply the reconstruction stencil. This organizes the computation without moving the Eulerian mesh.
