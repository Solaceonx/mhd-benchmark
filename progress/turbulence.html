Driven turbulence: the pilot

Hydrodynamic turbulence in a periodic box, stirred until it is statistically steady, in Tom Abel's GPU kernels and in RAMSES with one definition of the stirring. A first look: 4 Mach numbers, 2 grids. It also found a fault in RAMSES's driving, which is why RAMSES appears twice at the lower two Mach numbers.

How to read this page

Units: box length 1, mean density 1, sound speed 1. The gas is nearly isothermal (γ − 1 = 10⁻⁵, and 10⁻⁶ at Mach 30 and 50; no cooling), so the total energy rises at exactly the rate the driving puts it in: that slope is the power, and in a steady state the dissipation rate. The driving is a random acceleration on the largest scales (1 < k < 3) with memory time T = L/(2 M cs), scaled to the same strength at every moment in both codes. The strength was found once for each target Mach number and is used unchanged by every run, so a run is named by its target and the Mach number it reaches is a result.

Colour is the code: green Tom's kernels, purple RAMSES. Line style is the set-up. The paler purple is RAMSES as released: its projection of the driving takes the direction of a wave vector from the absolute values of its components, so a fifth of the power of its "solenoidal" force is compressive (measured from the force fields it saves). The full purple has those three lines corrected and is the RAMSES to compare with Tom's kernels; at Mach 30 and 50 only the corrected RAMSES ran. The green diamonds of figure 2 are the reverse test: Tom's Euler kernel driven with RAMSES's released projection. His Float16 Euler kernel is not here: it stops in its first steps, because it stores the energy flux in half precision and this gas has an internal energy of 10⁵.

Density images use the notebook's map for gas quantities (teal, dark where the density is low), as on the other pages.

1

Steady state

Rms Mach number (volume-weighted, with the sound speed of the gas at that time) against time in driving times T. Averages on this page are taken from 2 T on; the check is that the second half of that window agrees with the first.

In these runs: All 60 runs pass the check: the mean Mach number of the second half of the averaging window differs from the first half's by 0.0 % to 4.8 %, and the Mach number wanders by 1.6 % to 4.3 % about its mean. The paler curves (RAMSES as released) run lower from the start.

2

What RAMSES's projection does

Time-averaged Mach number and power taken up from the driving (the slope of the total energy), each relative to Tom's Euler kernel at the same Mach number and grid (mean of its two seeds). Every run of a Mach number uses the same driving strength.

In these runs: RAMSES as released sits −5.5 % to −2.3 % in Mach number and −19 % to −13 % in power against Tom's Euler kernel. With the projection corrected it is within −2.2 % to +0.6 % and −3 % to +7 %; two seeds of the Euler kernel differ by 2 % to 9 % in power. Tom's MHD kernel with zero field: −3.6 % to +1.3 % and −4 % to +4 %. The reverse test (diamonds): Tom's Euler kernel driven with RAMSES's released projection sits −4.3 % to −2.7 % and −19 % to −9 %, where released RAMSES sits. So the gap is the projection, not the solver.

3

Velocity spectra

Time-averaged velocity power spectra, multiplied by k² (flat for a field of shocks). Runs that share one driving only; Tom's kernels are the mean of two seeds. The two set-ups that run the same scheme in two codes are dashed with shifted dashes: where they coincide the line alternates green and purple.

In these runs: On 256³, Mach 3: the two HLL codes differ by at most 2 % at any k ≥ 4; at k = 64 RAMSES HLLC holds 2.2 times and the MHD kernel (LLF) 0.51 times the power of its HLL counterpart; Mach 10: the two HLL codes differ by at most 2 % at any k ≥ 4; at k = 64 RAMSES HLLC holds 1.9 times and the MHD kernel (LLF) 0.48 times the power of its HLL counterpart; Mach 30: the two HLL codes differ by at most 5 % at any k ≥ 4; at k = 64 RAMSES HLLC holds 1.8 times and the MHD kernel (LLF) 0.46 times the power of its HLL counterpart; Mach 50: the two HLL codes differ by at most 5 % at any k ≥ 4; at k = 64 RAMSES HLLC holds 1.8 times and the MHD kernel (LLF) 0.46 times the power of its HLL counterpart.

4

Compressive share, scale by scale

The part of the velocity power at each wavenumber that is compressive (along k). The driving acts at 1 < k < 3 and is meant to be solenoidal.

In these runs: The released projection shows only where the driving acts: Mach 3: at k = 2, 0.13 and 0.13 as released against 0.11 and 0.11 corrected and 0.11 for Tom's Euler kernel; Mach 10: at k = 2, 0.17 and 0.16 as released against 0.12 and 0.11 corrected and 0.13 for Tom's Euler kernel. Past the driven band the released and corrected curves of each solver join, and what separates the curves is the solver.

5

Density distribution

Time-averaged distribution of s = ln(ρ/⟨ρ⟩) by volume on the finer grid. The vertical line is the density floor.

In these runs: The width σ of the distribution of s gives b = √(exp σ² − 1)/Mach, about 1/3 for solenoidal driving in the literature. On 256³, Mach 3: b = 0.43 (Euler kernel), 0.42 (MHD kernel), 0.41 (RAMSES HLL), 0.41 (RAMSES HLLC), 0.45 (RAMSES HLL, released), 0.45 (RAMSES HLLC, released); Mach 10: b = 0.55 (Euler kernel), 0.50 (MHD kernel), 0.53 (RAMSES HLL), 0.46 (RAMSES HLLC), 0.57 (RAMSES HLL, released), 0.51 (RAMSES HLLC, released). From 128³ to 256³: Mach 3: 0.44 → 0.43 (Euler kernel), 0.41 → 0.41 (RAMSES HLLC); Mach 10: 0.63 → 0.55 (Euler kernel), 0.54 → 0.46 (RAMSES HLLC). Where it still moves with resolution it is not converged. At Mach 30 and 50 the distribution runs into the density floor of 10⁻⁶ in every code (the spike at the left): up to 2.0 % of the cells sit there, and in RAMSES up to 0.25 % are at or below zero. The width is then set by the floor, so no b is given for these Mach numbers.

6

Numerical dissipation

Effective Reynolds number v L/ν, with ν the viscosity that would turn the measured power into heat through the velocity gradients the grid holds: ν = power / (⟨ρ|∇×v|²⟩ + 4/3 ⟨ρ(∇·v)²⟩). It grows with resolution; on one grid a larger value means sharper gradients for the same dissipation. Short bars: the single seeds.

In these runs: From 128³ to 256³. Mach 3: Euler kernel 1158 → 2489, RAMSES HLL 1119 → 2423, RAMSES HLLC 1742 → 3776 (1.6 times HLL), MHD kernel 1111 → 2448 (−2 % against the Euler kernel); Mach 10: Euler kernel 1821 → 4151, RAMSES HLL 1820 → 4192, RAMSES HLLC 2626 → 6278 (1.5 times HLL), MHD kernel 1489 → 3558 (−14 % against the Euler kernel); Mach 30: Euler kernel 2067 → 4775, RAMSES HLL 1996 → 4745, RAMSES HLLC 2844 → 6922 (1.5 times HLL), MHD kernel 1575 → 3773 (−21 % against the Euler kernel); Mach 50: Euler kernel 2104 → 4855, RAMSES HLL 2033 → 4787, RAMSES HLLC 2826 → 7006 (1.5 times HLL), MHD kernel 1618 → 3777 (−22 % against the Euler kernel).

7

What it looks like

Density in the midplane at the end of each run (time 10 T) on the finer grid; light is dense, and each row has its own colour scale; cells below the density floor are drawn at it. The runs of a row have the same statistics of the driving but not the same random pattern (two codes, two random number generators; Tom's two kernels share theirs, and so do RAMSES's two solvers), so structures are not expected to match from code to code.
